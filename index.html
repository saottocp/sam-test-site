01 · A little context

I care about doing the work—and enjoying the people I do it with.

I came to Cal Poly wanting practical experience, not just a transcript. The best parts so far have happened when I volunteered for something I wasn’t completely ready for.

That mindset has taken me from classroom questions to real projects, team deadlines, research, and the occasional late night spent figuring out why something that worked yesterday suddenly does not.

I’m serious about being dependable, but I don’t think professionalism requires becoming boring. I bring curiosity, a sense of humor, and a willingness to ask the question everyone else is quietly wondering about.

02 · The path here

A few turns that shaped how I work.

Before

Finding momentum

Learning to be the person who raises a hand.

I did not arrive with a perfectly mapped-out plan. What I did have was a habit of getting curious, saying yes, and staying with a problem after the first solution failed. Small responsibilities became bigger ones because people learned they could count on me.

Curiosity · Follow-through · Teamwork
Year 01

Arriving at Cal Poly

Turning coursework into something tangible.

“Learn by doing” clicked with me immediately. I started treating assignments as chances to build judgment: clarify the actual problem, divide the work honestly, test the result, and explain it in a way another person can use.

Communication · Analysis · Iteration
Now

Taking on more

Choosing projects with a little uncertainty in them.

My favorite work now sits just beyond what I already know. It asks me to learn a tool, understand someone else’s perspective, and make progress without pretending I have every answer. That stretch is where I have done my best work.
